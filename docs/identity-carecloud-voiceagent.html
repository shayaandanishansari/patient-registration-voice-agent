Voice AI Agent — Patient Registration System

Identity of CareCloud VoiceAgent

Which phone line this agent actually is, and how that was decided.

Why this document exists

The assessment brief is precise about what the agent must do and vague about what the agent is. It specifies a registration flow, which implies a narrow intake line. It then offers appointment scheduling as a bonus, which implies something closer to the general hospital switchboard most people have actually called.

Those two readings produce different products. Before writing the system prompt, it was worth establishing how patient phone lines are really organised in the United States, so the agent's scope would be a deliberate choice rather than a side effect of whichever requirement was read last. This document records that research and the decision it led to.

How the patient and the hospital are connected

In the United States a patient is normally attached to a physician's practice rather than to a hospital. A PCP (Primary Care Physician) or a specialist is the entry point to care. The hospital is where ordered procedures happen, and patients generally arrive there because a physician sent them.

That ordering matters, because it explains why registration exists as its own phone call:

  1. A physician orders or schedules the service Surgery, imaging, lab work or an admission is arranged by the practice, often before the hospital has spoken to the patient at all.
  2. Patient Access pre-registers the patient The hospital's Patient Access department collects demographics, insurance and emergency contact details ahead of the visit, by phone or online.
  3. The patient arrives and is checked in Because the record already exists, arrival is a short confirmation step rather than a paperwork session.

So the registration line is not the front door to the hospital. It is one step inside a process that began somewhere else. By the time this call happens, someone has already decided the patient is coming. That is precisely why a phone line can be this narrow and still be a real job: Johns Hopkins, Torrance Memorial, UAMS and Guthrie all publish dedicated pre-registration numbers staffed by their Patient Access teams.

The lines a patient can call

Everything in a hospital contact centre is organised around one division: clinical versus non-clinical. It decides who may sit in the seat, which protocols apply and where the liability falls. Non-clinical staff escalate anything symptom-related rather than answering it.

Nurse triage and nurse advice
RNs (Registered Nurses) assess symptoms against written clinical decision trees and recommend a level of care. Kaiser Permanente's Nurse Advice Line and the VA's (Veterans Affairs) Telephone Triage Service are the reference examples. This is the highest-risk call type in the building, and it is the reason the clinical boundary is drawn so firmly.

Organisations are described as either administrative-only, covering scheduling, message routing and general enquiries, or integrated, which adds after-hours answering, care coordination or nurse triage. Large health systems increasingly centralise all of these into a single contact centre rather than routing each through its own department.

Two conventions worth copying

Authenticate on at least two identifiers

Human agents confirm identity using two or more identifiers before touching a record. This is not just etiquette. Verifying the caller before disclosing any account or clinical information is a HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act) Security Rule obligation for telephone interactions involving PHI (Protected Health Information).

It is also why the brief's duplicate-detection bonus is implemented here as identity verification instead. Reading a name back to anyone who recites a phone number turns the line into a lookup service for other people's records.

Open with the emergency disclaimer

Patient-facing lines open with a standing instruction before anything else happens:

If this is a life-threatening emergency, hang up and dial 911.

One sentence, and it establishes immediately that this line is not clinical.

The failure mode this all guards against

The named danger in contact centre practice is a caller believing they have reached one kind of line when they have actually reached another. Someone describing chest pain to a scheduler has not just wasted a call. They have been delayed.

An agent that sounds capable of anything invites exactly that mistake. An agent that states its scope plainly, and hands off cleanly when a caller needs something else, does not.

What this agent is

CareCloud VoiceAgent is a pre-registration and patient access line. It answers as an intake coordinator for a patient whose care has already been arranged elsewhere, collects the demographic and insurance dataset, verifies identity before touching an existing record, reads everything back for confirmation, and writes to the patient database.

That identity resolves the tension in the brief. Appointment scheduling is not a second persona bolted on: central scheduling and patient access are routinely combined into one desk, and in practice scheduling normally comes first, with registration following. Offering to book a first appointment at the end of a registration call is the same coordinator doing an adjacent part of the same job.

It also settles what the agent deliberately will not do. Advising which doctor to see, interpreting symptoms or assessing urgency is nurse triage. That work sits behind a licensing boundary, and several states have now written the boundary into statute. The agent names its scope and escalates instead.

One line, well defined. The scope was chosen from how these lines actually work, not trimmed to fit the time limit.

The same directory, published by two health systems

The split above is not a theoretical model. It is visible on the contact pages of most large health systems, where each function is given its own number rather than sitting behind a single switchboard.

Neither page offers a single general-purpose line that does everything. The caller is expected to know roughly what they need, and the organisation optimises each line for its one job. That is the pattern this agent follows.
